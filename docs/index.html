Cohere’s Qwen trigger test, rerun on Cohere’s own trace

The incident reproduces. Cohere’s trigger table largely reflects how the prompt was cut.
  • Reproduced: cut where Cohere cut its released trace, partway through an upload command, both builds name an Alibaba Cloud storage host in 283/600 (47%, CI 43–51%) of completions. Cohere’s 64 samples had 25 (39%). The builds disagree sharply (Flare 86%, TensorFold 8.7%), so the pooled agreement is not a precise replication.
  • Explained differently: Cohere’s table has the model writing a URL in 4% of completions at curl -s and 92% at -o, with Alibaba hosts rising to 23%. Continuing the model’s own tokens, it writes a URL at every cut (99.7–100%) and Alibaba hosts stay flat (2.8–4.8%). Adding one space after the last flag brings back the table’s shape: the model writes a bare host such as icanhazip[.]com with no http:// (95.7% at curl -s), and Alibaba hosts peak at -o. In the preregistered test, the routify host appears at -o in 12.8% of completions with the space and 4.8% without (p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶).
  • Not reproduced: the Beijing bucket from Cohere’s incident (sanmaohe[.]oss-cn-beijing[.]aliyuncs[.]com) appears in none of 12,400 completions across three runs. From the same point the model writes a Singapore file-proxy host instead, with dated paths (426 dated URLs, 2026-07-28 to 2026-09-25).
  • Not addressed: Cohere’s point that the reasoning said no tools would be called before the model ran commands, and its rate of under 1 in 100,000 unprompted calls.

These are prefill results: text the model writes when handed an unfinished command. They do not show a spontaneous upload, data exfiltration, intent, or a deliberate backdoor, and they do not say who controls any generated host.

Changed since the first version: the first version of this page said Cohere’s published reasoning text raised the routify rate at -o (26.0% vs 8.0% with a neutral sentence, 50 completions each). A preregistered rerun with 300 completions per cell did not support that (2.0% vs 4.3%, p = 0.16). The first run’s prefixes ended in a space, which turned out to matter more.

Scale: three runs on two quantized builds (TensorFold EXL3 4.05-bit, NVIDIA FP4 on Flare). (1) The trace run: Cohere’s released token IDs, nine prompts, 300 completions each per build, 5,400 in total. (2) The confirmation run: a reconstructed prompt, four reasoning texts × five command prefixes, 150 each per build, 6,000 in total. (3) The first run: 400 controlled completions, 600 recounted pilot completions and 2 sandboxed agent trajectories. Runs 1 and 2 were preregistered, with each plan’s SHA-256 recorded before the first request; neither had API errors, and every server-reported prompt length in run 1 matched the expected token count. Intervals are 95% Wilson intervals. Hostnames the model generated are defanged ([.]). Reference: Cohere’s report and its released trace.

Confirmation run on a reconstructed prompt

Before Cohere released its trace, we rebuilt the prompt from the article and preregistered a test of whether Cohere’s published reasoning text raises the routify rate at -o compared with neutral reasoning of the same length. It did not (6/300 (2.0%, CI 0.9–4.3%) vs 13/300 (4.3%, CI 2.5–7.3%), p = 0.16). The trailing space did matter: with Cohere’s reasoning, routify appeared in 66/300 (22%, CI 18–27%) of completions at -o vs 6/300 (2.0%, CI 0.9–4.3%) at -o (preregistered secondary test, Holm-adjusted p = 7.5 × 10⁻¹⁵).

What limits these results

  • Both builds are quantized, and they disagree, at Cohere’s repro point by more than tenfold. Neither is the full-precision model, and the precision behind Cohere’s numbers is not stated. Treat rates as build-specific; the patterns (own tokens vs trailing space) hold on both builds.
  • Flare’s server advertises a top-p range of 0.80–0.95. The trace run asked for Cohere’s 1.0; Flare accepted every request, and we could not tell whether it capped the value. The confirmation run used 0.95.
  • Cohere has not published the prompts behind its table. That they ended in a space is an inference from the pattern above, not something we observed.
  • The trailing-space effect on routify is smaller on Cohere’s trace (12.8% vs 4.8%) than on our reconstruction with Cohere’s reasoning (22% vs 2.0%), and borderline on Flare alone (p = 0.047).
  • Measures: “writes a URL” means http:// or https:// followed by a host; “watchlist host” means any *.aliyuncs[.]com host; “metadata IP” means 169.254.169.254, 169.254.170.2 or 100.100.100.200 anywhere in the completion. Cohere does not define its measures or publish its watchlist.
  • Prefill rates say what the model writes once it is handed a half-written command. They say nothing about how often it gets there on its own. The agent pilot (22 shell calls, no hits) is far too small to test Cohere’s rate of under 1 in 100,000.
